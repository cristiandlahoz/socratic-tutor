Audience: PUCMM thesis defense committee evaluating Tutor Crow as a pedagogically constrained AI tutor for introductory programming.

Objective: present Tutor Crow as a deliberate educational system, not just an LLM wrapper, and leave the committee with a clear argument: the value is guided reasoning, not answer generation.

Narrative arc:
- Start from the instructional problem in ICC-101.
- Show why generic AI is misaligned with learning goals.
- Establish the pedagogical principles that shape the system.
- Translate those principles into architecture and the ICC-101 tutoring flow.
- Show demo/evaluation/finding slides as proof of seriousness.
- Close on limitations, contribution, and next-step credibility.

Slide list:
1. Cover: Tutor Crow and the core thesis.
2. Problem framing: cognitive load, unrestricted AI, and the need for guided support.
3. Design principles: scaffolding, Socratic guidance, and misconception-aware feedback.
4. Objectives: knowledge base, tutoring behavior, and evaluation.
5. System architecture: learner input to guided response pipeline.
6. ICC-101 use case: how the tutoring cycle behaves on an algorithms misconception.
7. Demo: what a good interaction looks like.
8. Evaluation strategy: what counts as success.
9. Main findings: strongest outcomes from the proposed validation logic.
10. Limitations: scope, language, infrastructure, and role boundaries.
11. Contributions: what this project adds academically and practically.
12. Next steps: how to make the system stronger after defense.

Visual system:
- Warm paper background with dark ink typography and one restrained blue accent.
- Serif-led titles, clean sans body, generous margins, thin rules, sparse ornament.
- Cards only where they help comparison; diagrams for system/process slides.
- Footer kept minimal: institution and thesis context.

Editability plan:
- All visible text remains native editable PowerPoint text.
- Architecture and tutoring-cycle diagrams are native shapes and connectors.
- No screenshots for content slides.
